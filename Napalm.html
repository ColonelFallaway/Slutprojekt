The Development of Napalm

Napalm is a flammable gel used in incendiary weapons. It was first developed during World War II and became widely known for its use in warfare because it sticks to surfaces and burns at extremely high temperatures. The substance is made by mixing gasoline with thickening agents, creating a sticky fuel that spreads fire over large areas.

Napalm During the Vietnam War

During the Vietnam War, napalm became one of the most controversial military weapons in the world. Images and reports showing the devastating effects of napalm on civilians led to international criticism and protests. The weapon caused severe burns, destruction of villages, and long-lasting psychological trauma for many people affected by the conflict.

International Restrictions on Napalm

Today, the use of incendiary weapons such as napalm is heavily restricted under international law. Many countries signed agreements limiting their use, especially in areas with civilian populations. Napalm remains a symbol of the destructive impact of modern warfare and is often discussed in history, politics, and ethics studies.
