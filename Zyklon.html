Chemistry and composition

Zyklon B was a cyanide-based pesticide. Its active component was hydrogen cyanide (HCN), a highly toxic chemical that interferes with cellular respiration. The pellets or granules were designed to release gas when exposed to air.

Industrial and agricultural use

Before and outside its wartime use, Zyklon B was used as a fumigant to control insects and pests in places such as warehouses, ships, grain storage areas, and buildings. Fumigation chemicals were widely used in the early 20th century for public health and pest-control purposes.

Historical significance

Zyklon B is most widely associated with the Holocaust, where it was used in gas chambers at several Nazi concentration and extermination camps. Because of this role, it remains a central subject in studies of history, ethics, genocide, and human rights.
