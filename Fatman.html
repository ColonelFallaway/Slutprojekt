Design and technology

Fat Man was an implosion-type nuclear weapon. It used plutonium as its fissile material and relied on precisely arranged explosive charges that compressed the core inward. This design was more complex than earlier nuclear weapon concepts and represented a major development in nuclear physics and engineering.

Historical use

Fat Man was dropped on the Japanese city of Nagasaki on August 9, 1945, during World War II. It was the second and last nuclear weapon used in warfare and played a major role in the final stage of the war.

Political and ethical impact

The use of Fat Man had long-term consequences beyond the war itself. It influenced international politics, contributed to the nuclear arms race during the Cold War, and continues to raise ethical questions about warfare, civilian casualties, deterrence, and the role of nuclear weapons in modern society.
